# Project: Metoprolol Side Effect Tracking via Smartwatch Data

## Objective
To utilize the Google Pixel Watch 3 and the Fitbit ecosystem to monitor, quantify, and manage common side effects of Metoprolol, including "heavy legs," afternoon fatigue slumps, and vivid REM-rebound dreams.

---

## Phase 1: The Heart Rate "Idle" Test
Metoprolol is a beta-blocker designed to lower the heart rate and blood pressure. However, if the heart rate drops too low, it can lead to physical sluggishness or a "heavy-leg" sensation. The watch can help identify your true "idle speed."

### The Morning Baseline
* **Action:** Use the *Fitbit Today* app on your watch to check your Resting Heart Rate (RHR) the exact moment you wake up, before getting out of bed.
* **Why it matters:** If your RHR is consistently dropping below the 50–55 bpm threshold, it biologically explains why that first morning walk feels like moving through mud. Your heart isn't pumping enough oxygenated blood to your extremities to match your intended exertion.
* **Data to log:** Daily Morning RHR.

### The 6-Hour Slump Check
* **Action:** Manually check and log your heart rate during the predictable 2:00 PM "slump."
* **Comparison:** Compare this specific slump heart rate to your heart rate during times when you are actively busy and "riding it out" (e.g., working, talking, or moving around).
* **Why it matters:** This establishes a concrete delta between your baseline activity heart rate and your medicated "crash" heart rate, showing exactly how much your cardiovascular system is being suppressed during these fatigue spikes.

---

## Phase 2: Sleep Architecture & "Banger" Dreams
Beta-blockers like Metoprolol are known to cross the blood-brain barrier, which can interfere with natural melatonin production and disrupt REM sleep cycles, leading to intensely vivid or bizarre "TV show" dreams.

### REM Monitoring
* **Action:** Review your *Sleep Stages* graph in the Fitbit mobile app every morning.
* **What to look for:** Look for heavily fragmented REM sleep blocks or abnormally frequent "Awake" spikes (the red lines on the Fitbit graph).
* **Why it matters:** Seeing fragmented REM visually confirms why your dreams feel so memorable and exhaustive. It validates that the dreams are a physiological side effect, not just random occurrences.

### Nightly Disruption & Restoration
* **Action:** Track your daily "Restoration" score within your Sleep Profile.
* **Why it matters:** If you are waking up feeling completely exhausted despite sleeping 8 hours—and having wild dreams—your body isn't entering deep, restorative sleep.
* **Doctor Check-in:** Compile a week's worth of Restoration scores and REM fragmentation data. Use this objective data to discuss with your doctor whether adjusting your dosage timing or introducing a low-dose melatonin supplement is appropriate.

---

## Phase 3: Readiness & Cardio Load
The feeling of "heavy legs" isn't just in your head; it's a mismatch between your cardiovascular output and muscular demand. Fitbit's advanced metrics can help you pace yourself.

### Cardio Load Monitoring
* **Action:** Monitor your *Cardio Load* metric on days when your legs feel exceptionally heavy.
* **What it reveals:** This metric shows how hard your cardiovascular system is working compared to your historical baseline. On heavy-leg days, you might see that your heart is actually being "under-taxed" because the medication is capping your heart rate, forcing your muscles to work harder without the usual blood flow boost.

### Daily Readiness Score Optimization
* **Action:** Check your *Daily Readiness Score* each morning before planning your day's activity level.
* **How to use it:** * **Low Score:** A clear physiological signal that your body is currently struggling with the medication's adjustment or recovering from a poor night of disrupted sleep. Use this as empirical justification to take it easier, opt for light stretching instead of a brisk walk, and give yourself grace.
    * **High Score:** Your body has adapted well to the recent doses; safe to push slightly harder on physical tasks.

## Next Steps
1.  **Create a 14-Day Log:** Use a spreadsheet or a physical notebook to record Morning RHR, 2 PM Slump HR, Sleep Score, and Readiness Score for two straight weeks.
2.  **Note the Dose Timing:** Record exactly when you take your Metoprolol to see how it correlates with the 2 PM slump and nightly dream intensity.
3.  **Consultation:** Export or screenshot these trends to show your cardiologist or primary care physician. Objective data removes the guesswork from side effect management and empowers better medical decisions.
